# Evolutionary ecology

## Portable structures

### 1. Reciprocal niche-construction feedback
**Relation:** population state or traits → environmental modification → changed selection conditions → later population evolution. Evolution and ecological change can therefore be mutually causal within the same time window, rather than neatly ordered as independent stages.

**Assumptions:** The population measurably modifies an environmental feature; that feature affects subsequent selection or inheritance-linked trait change; the relevant ecological and evolutionary processes overlap in time. The loop can include other species or ecosystem processes.

Source: Post & Palkovacs (2009), “Eco-evolutionary feedbacks in community and ecosystem ecology: interactions between the ecological theatre and the evolutionary play,” https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2009.0012. Abstract read; it defines the niche-construction/feedback loop and surveys five systems. See `retrieval_notes.md` and `post_palkovacs_abstract.json`.

### 2. Effect-relative timescale criterion
**Relation:** ecological change = contribution associated with evolutionary change + contribution associated with non-evolutionary drivers. Evolution counts as “rapid” relative to a specified ecological outcome when its contribution is large enough to measurably affect that outcome during the same interval; generation-normalized trait rates alone do not determine ecological importance.

**Assumptions:** The ecological outcome and observation interval are specified; evolutionary and non-evolutionary contributions can be meaningfully distinguished or estimated; relevant interactions are represented well enough for the decomposition to be informative.

Source: Hairston et al. (2005), “Rapid evolution and the convergence of ecological and evolutionary time,” https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2005.00812.x. Abstract read; it proposes this effect-relative definition and decomposition, with examples whose relative contributions differ by system. See `retrieval_notes.md` and `hairston_abstract.json`.

### 3. Causal attribution across coupled change
**Relation:** when trait frequencies and ecological conditions shift together, ask how much of the focal ecological trajectory is attributable to evolutionary change versus demographic or abiotic change, and how the attribution changes when the evolutionary response itself feeds back on conditions.

**Assumptions:** There is a defined focal ecological process and interval; candidate causal drivers are observable or modelable; attribution is not inferred from covariation alone. This is a synthesis of the two source structures, not a claim that either source supplies a universally identified decomposition.

Sources: Post & Palkovacs (2009) and Hairston et al. (2005), cited above.

## Open tensions

- Feedback loops are conceptually reciprocal, yet field time series often leave direction and causal order ambiguous: the same environmental shift can select traits and arise from those traits, while external drivers may move both.
- “Rapid enough to matter” is outcome-relative. A trait change may strongly affect one ecological variable and barely affect another; a conclusion can change with the chosen spatial scale, time window, or counterfactual baseline.
- Evolutionary and ecological changes can occur on overlapping timescales, but the feedback may be delayed, nonlinear, or mediated through interacting species and ecosystem processes. It remains difficult to decide when a simple two-link loop is an adequate representation and when the added links change the predicted direction of change.

# Economics and mechanism design: retrieval notes

Retrieved 2026-11-04. Source pages were accessed directly and saved as HTML in
`source_notes/`. These are official Nobel Prize press-release summaries, used as
review sources rather than as primary research articles.

## 1. Mechanism design theory (2007 Nobel Prize press release)

- URL: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2007/press-release/
- Saved page: `source_notes/nobel_2007.html`
- Read excerpt: “information about individual preferences and available production
  technologies is usually dispersed among many actors who may use their private
  information to further their own interests.” The page says mechanism design
  studies optimal allocation mechanisms while accounting for incentives and private
  information, and identifies trading mechanisms, regulation, and voting as uses.
- Source claim: allocation institutions must be evaluated with actors' information
  and incentives in view.
- Interpretation used in the card: formulate a design problem as a mapping from
  reports/actions to outcomes, then ask which outcomes are implementable when
  participants have private information and strategic motives.

## 2. Stable allocations and market design (2012 Nobel Prize press release)

- URL: https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/economic-sciences/2012/press-release/
- Saved page: `source_notes/nobel_2012.html`
- Read excerpt: a matching is stable if no two agents prefer each other to their
  assigned counterparts; the Gale-Shapley algorithm guarantees a stable matching
  and limits incentives to manipulate. The page also notes that design choices can
  systematically benefit one side, and that practical reforms adapt the algorithm
  to circumstances and ethical restrictions, including no side payments.
- Source claim: stability, strategic behavior, distributional effects, and local
  institutional restrictions all matter to matching design.
- Interpretation used in the card: an institution can have multiple relevant
  performance criteria whose tradeoffs are exposed by specifying who can deviate,
  what counts as a blocking pair, and which transfers or choices are allowed.

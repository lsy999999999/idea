# Economics and mechanism design

## Portable structures

1. **Strategic implementation under dispersed private information.** Model an
   institution as rules mapping reports or actions to outcomes. The design question
   becomes whether a desirable allocation can be attained when participants have
   private information and their own objectives, rather than only which allocation
   is desirable.
2. **Stability as absence of mutually profitable deviations.** In matching, an
   outcome is stable when no pair would both prefer to leave their current
   assignments for each other. This makes the set of feasible deviations part of
   the definition of a satisfactory outcome.
3. **Institutional rules distribute incentives and advantage.** Procedural choices
   and local restrictions can alter manipulation incentives, systematically favor
   one side, or exclude otherwise available mechanisms. Formal properties need to
   be interpreted with the institution's actual constraints in view.

## Open tensions

- Efficiency can conflict with truthfulness or resistance to strategic behavior
  under private information.
- Stability rules out blocking pairs but does not guarantee fairness or maximal
  welfare; design choices can shift benefits between sides.
- Practical or ethical restrictions can exclude mechanisms with attractive formal
  properties, changing what counts as a good design.

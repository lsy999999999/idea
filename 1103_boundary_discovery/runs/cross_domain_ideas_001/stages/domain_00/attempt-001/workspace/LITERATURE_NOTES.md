# Non-equilibrium statistical physics: retrieval notes

Access date for all sources: 2026-11-04.

## 1. Seifert (2012), review

Title: *Stochastic thermodynamics, fluctuation theorems, and molecular machines*.
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.4176 (full HTML read at https://arxiv.org/html/1205.4176).
Local retrieved text: `seifert.html` (full HTML review).

Read the abstract, introductory framing, and sections on fluctuation theorems and
coarse-graining. The review defines work, heat, and entropy production on individual
stochastic trajectories in well-defined non-equilibrium ensembles. In its basic Markovian
setting, thermodynamic consistency imposes local detailed balance on transition rates or
noise. A master theorem yields integral and detailed fluctuation theorems. The review also
has a dedicated discussion of memory effects and coarse-graining, indicating that the
trajectory-level formulation depends on what variables and dynamics are resolved.

## 2. Bertini et al. (2015), review

Title: *Macroscopic fluctuation theory*.
URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.6466 (full HTML read at https://arxiv.org/html/1404.6466).
Local retrieved text: `mft.html` (full HTML review).

Read the abstract, hydrodynamic description, fundamental formula, and density/current
large-deviation sections. The review formulates driven diffusive systems through local
density and current with a continuity equation and constitutive current. It gives a
space-time path cost for joint density-current histories; unlikely histories can be
expressed as the cost of an effective extra field subject to the conservation constraint.
The review relates time reversal to current fluctuation symmetries and discusses nonlocal
quasi-potentials, long-range correlations, and dynamical phase transitions. Its diffusive
hydrodynamic formulation assumes scale separation and a specified constitutive law; the
simple version discussed early in the paper treats a single conserved quantity.

## 3. Evans, Cohen & Morriss (1993), primary paper record

Title: *Probability of second law violations in shearing steady states*.
URL: https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.71.2401 (APS abstract and article record read).
Local retrieved text: `evans.html` (APS article page; full text marked closed access).

The APS abstract says the authors define an invariant measure for finite trajectory
segments and derive a finite-time expression for the probability ratio of stress
fluctuations that reverse sign in a sheared non-equilibrium steady state; simulations
support the formula. This primary example grounds fluctuation relations in trajectory
statistics beyond linear response. The available page exposed the abstract and metadata,
not the closed full text, so no full-text claims are made here.

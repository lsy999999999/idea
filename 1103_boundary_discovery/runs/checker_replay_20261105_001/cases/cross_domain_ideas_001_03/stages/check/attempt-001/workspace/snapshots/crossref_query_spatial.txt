Feedback between game strategies and environmental states is common in human-natural systems. Existing studies have reported several interesting dynamical behaviors by studying the impact of environmental state on strategy selection. In this paper, we construct a new characterization of environmental states, assuming that environmental states are determined by local and global environmental states. Based on this, we construct a spatial multiagent coevolutionary model to describe the evolution of populations and analyze the model's dynamics by means of Monte Carlo simulations and mean-field approximation. We find the spatial structure can enhance system stability, preventing the emergence of complex dynamics such as chaos. Furthermore, we incorporate diffusion mechanism into the model. Results show resource diffusion accelerates switching and weakening differences. Numerical results further indicate structure significantly affects stability: the system self-organizes into stable structures analogous to Turing patterns, thus enhancing stability. This study investigates mechanisms which influence system stability, offering new insights into understanding real-world human-natural systems.

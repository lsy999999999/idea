Supplementary Video 1 (download MP4
)
Wave coarsening in a 2D non-reciprocal mechatronic metamaterial. A mechatronic unit cell deforms non-reciprocally under load. Beyond the Hopf bifurcation, limit cycle oscillations emerge. A mechatronic metamaterial consisting of 37 unit cells spontaneously deforms as shear waves coarsen from a small wavelength, period and amplitude to system-spanning deformations with large period and amplitude.
Supplementary Video 2 (download MP4
)
Spatial correlations suggest power-law coarsening. Numerical simulations of equation (2) in two dimensions reveal a gradual, self-similar increase of the characteristic wavelength and amplitude of oscillations. This video visualizes the evolution of the inverse correlation length
ξ
−1
and elastic energy 〈|
S
|〉, highlighting the power-law scaling of wave coarsening.
Supplementary Video 3 (download MP4
)
Wave coarsening in a 1D elastic chain with non-reciprocal interactions. A one-dimensional elastic ring of 24 torsional and non-reciprocal unit cells is driven beyond the instability threshold. Initially, high-mode-number travelling waves appear, which progressively merge into longer wavelength, larger-amplitude oscillations. Eventually, the system coarsens into a stable low-frequency time-crystalline state.
Supplementary Video 4 (download MP4
)
Frictional arrest of wave coarsening and edge waves. This video shows the 2D metamaterial of Fig.
1
deforming on an unlubricated surface. Although waves coarsen from their initial high wave number instability, waves in the steady state do not span the system size, and leave a stable strain defect visible. Large-scale simulations of the metamaterial show that edge waves eventually dominate the dynamics. When friction is introduced, coarsening is arrested, yielding steady counter-rotating and edge-localized waves.

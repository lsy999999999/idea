# Cross-disciplinary research ideas

The objective is to propose new research ideas. We do not need to solve them now.

Work across substantially different disciplines. Discover a useful correspondence between
their mechanisms, constraints, or ways of representing a problem, and use it to formulate a
research question or principle that neither field's usual framing makes obvious. The domains
in this pilot are starting inputs, not a claim that this particular combination is optimal.

Preserve ambitious, unresolved proposals. Immediate feasibility, a short proof, accessible
data, software implementation, and experimental verification are not selection criteria.
Difficulty alone does not establish originality either. A clear research direction and an
understandable conceptual move are sufficient to propose a candidate. Equations are optional.

After saving the complete idea batch, a final checker makes only two judgments:

1. Has a very similar research direction already been explored? If so, mark it already done,
   even if details differ. Read sources and compare the underlying question and approach.
2. Is there an obvious mathematical or logical error? A direction that broadly makes sense
   passes. Lack of a solution or evidence is not a mathematical error.

Do not conduct scientific experiments or try to solve the ideas. Do not import earlier river,
SQLite, RNA, or other project candidates. Inspiration from actual literature is appropriate.
The final check is a bounded screen, not proof of global novelty. Preserve all proposed ideas
and their two separate check results, including ideas judged already done.

# Research Idea: Degradation-Mode-Aware, Condition-Normalized EIS for SOH Estimation

## Core idea
Develop an interpretable estimator that separates reversible measurement-condition effects (temperature and state of charge, SOC) from persistent aging information in electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), while preserving degradation-mode information rather than collapsing the spectrum to a single health factor. The estimator would use a physically motivated impedance representation (for example, bulk, SEI/interface, charge-transfer, and diffusion-related components where identifiable), normalize or condition on temperature and SOC, and predict capacity-based SOH together with a calibrated uncertainty estimate. A secondary diagnostic output would indicate whether the impedance pattern is more consistent with conductivity loss, loss of active material, or loss of lithium inventory, but only where the data support that distinction.

This is an early-stage research direction, not a claim of novelty: the supplied abstracts describe existing temperature/SOC compensation, circuit-parameter and machine-learning approaches, fast EIS acquisition, and EIS-based degradation-mode analysis. The proposed contribution is to test their integration as a constrained, interpretable estimation problem and explicitly evaluate whether mode-sensitive impedance information improves SOH generalization beyond condition-normalized scalar features.

## Hypothesis
Across cells and operating conditions, a model that first accounts for temperature and SOC effects on interpretable impedance features, then estimates SOH while retaining a degradation-mode-sensitive representation, will have lower out-of-condition SOH error and better-calibrated uncertainty than (a) an uncorrected impedance-feature regressor and (b) a condition-corrected regressor that uses only aggregate resistance/health factors. Its advantage should be largest when the test condition differs from the training condition or when different aging modes produce similar capacity loss but distinct impedance changes.

## Why this is scientifically useful
- It addresses two linked problems highlighted by the supplied sources: EIS features vary with temperature/SOC, and capacity loss can arise from interacting aging mechanisms that are not captured by SOH alone.
- It makes a falsifiable distinction between condition compensation and useful aging signal. If normalization improves random-split results but not condition-held-out performance, or removes useful aging information, that would expose a limitation of simple compensation.
- It tests whether degradation-mode structure contributes information beyond a capacity/SOH scalar, potentially linking diagnosis to battery lifetime control and design decisions.
- The result could clarify the trade-off among physical interpretability, estimation accuracy, and practical measurement flexibility. It does not assume that individual modes can always be identified uniquely from EIS.

## Minimal research question and comparison
Does a condition-normalized, mode-sensitive impedance representation improve capacity-based SOH estimation under held-out temperature/SOC conditions and held-out aging trajectories, relative to raw-feature and aggregate-feature baselines?

A later experimental study should use matched cells with reference capacity measurements, EIS across controlled SOC and temperature points, and documented aging protocols. Comparisons should use cell- or trajectory-level splits (not random spectrum-level splits), report MAE/RMSE and uncertainty calibration, and include ablations for condition correction and mode-sensitive features. Any degradation-mode label should be independently grounded in protocol or diagnostic evidence rather than inferred from SOH alone. These are proposed design requirements, not experiments performed here.

## Source basis and limits
The supplied `case_context.json` provides abstracts and metadata only. Relevant source entries include:
- Zhang et al., “Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy: A New Chapter in the Fast and Accurate Estimation of the State of Health for Lithium-Ion Batteries”: EIS spans frequencies and reflects aging; reviews measurement and estimation approaches.
- Iurilli, Brivio, and Wood, “On the use of electrochemical impedance spectroscopy to characterize and model the aging phenomena of lithium-ion batteries: a critical review”: aging test conditions affect spectra; reviews links between spectra and degradation mechanisms and circuit models.
- Q.-M. Zhang et al., “Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy based State of Health Estimation for Lithium-ion Battery Considering Temperature and State of Charge Effect”: abstract reports a probabilistic method using charge-transfer resistance, temperature, and SOC, with errors around 4% overall and 1.29% in a stated condition.
- Choi et al., “Modeling and Applications of Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS) for Lithium-ion Batteries”: abstract relates bulk, charge-transfer, interface, and diffusion elements to SOC, temperature, and SOH.
- Pastor-Fernández et al., “A Comparison between Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy and Incremental Capacity-Differential Voltage as Li-ion Diagnostic Techniques to Identify and Quantify the Effects of Degradation Modes within Battery Management Systems”: abstract discusses conductivity loss, loss of active material, and loss of lithium inventory, and reports correlated insights from EIS and IC-DV in its experimental case.
- Wang et al., “Estimation of state of health of lithium-ion batteries based on charge transfer resistance considering different temperature and state of charge”: abstract describes converting charge-transfer resistance across conditions for SOH estimation.

No online search or source-text verification was performed. The context contains abstracts rather than full methods, datasets, uncertainty analyses, or detailed limitations. Reported performance figures above are attributed to the abstracts and are not directly comparable across studies. The supplied evidence does not establish that the proposed integration is novel, that degradation modes are identifiable from EIS alone, or that it will outperform existing methods. Those points require full-paper review and controlled experiments.

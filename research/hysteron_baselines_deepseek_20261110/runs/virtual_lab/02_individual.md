*Model assumption.* Treat the bars as rigid, the springs as linear, the drive as quasistatic, and friction/inertia as negligible. Let the coupling attachment point on rotor \(i\) be
\[
\mathbf R_i(\theta_i)=\mathbf p_i+\ell_i\,\mathbf e(\theta_i),
\]
with coupling vector \(\mathbf d=\mathbf R_i-\mathbf R_j\), \(r=|\mathbf d|\), and rest length \(r_0\). The coupling torque on rotor \(i\) is
\[
\tau_i^{(c)}=-k_c\left(1-\frac{r_0}{r}\right)\mathbf d\cdot \ell_i\mathbf e'(\theta_i).
\]
At a hard stop \(\theta_i^\sigma\), switching occurs when the net torque into the stop vanishes:
\[
\gamma_i^\sigma(s_j,\ell)=\gamma_{i0}^\sigma-\frac{\tau_i^{(c)}(\theta_i^\sigma,s_j,\ell)}{A_i},
\]
where \(A_i\) converts drive displacement into torque. Expanding about the stop gives, to leading order,
\[
\tau_i^{(c)}(\theta_i^\sigma,s_j,\ell)=\tau_{i0}^{(c)}+\ell^2\left[a_i^\sigma+b_i^\sigma s_j\right]+O(\ell^3).
\]
Hence
\[
\gamma_i^\sigma=m_{i0}(\ell)+J_{ij}(\ell)s_j+\sigma h_i(\ell),
\]
with
\[
h_i(\ell)=h_{i0}-c_i\ell^2+O(\ell^4),\qquad
J_{ij}(\ell)=\beta_i\ell^2+O(\ell^4).
\]
The observed common \(L_c\) means \(c_i/h_{i0}\simeq L_c^{-2}\), but \(\beta_i\) is rotor-specific. The \(J\) term comes from the cross term \((\partial r/\partial\theta_i)(\partial r/\partial\theta_j)\); therefore its sign is controlled by the relative sign of the two lever projections and by the preload factor \(1-r_0/r\). The \(h\) term is the diagonal, even-in-lever contribution; its negative sign in pair A is permitted by tensile preload and stop geometry. This replaces “negative-curvature barrier collapse” by a zero-torque condition at fixed stop: the threshold moves because the coupling torque changes, not because a local potential barrier is destroyed.

*Conditional consequence.* If this geometry dominates, a same-device parallel-to-crossed swap at fixed \(\ell\), stops, springs and attachment distances flips the sign of \(J_{ij}\) while leaving \(h_i(\ell)\) and \(m_{i0}(\ell)\) approximately unchanged, up to the \(\pm0.1\) cm threshold uncertainty. If instead \(h\) changes strongly, or \(|J|\) changes while its sign does not, then preload, rest-length or stop-geometry differences dominate and the geometric-crossing explanation is rejected for that device.

*Scope.* Pair A supports the \(\ell^2\) width collapse and state-dependent bias. Pair B supports only a conditional sign reversal. Network C’s repeated eight-event cycle is consistent with a stable ordered response, but absent reversal rows and timestamps it cannot distinguish independent near-degenerate thresholds from an avalanche.

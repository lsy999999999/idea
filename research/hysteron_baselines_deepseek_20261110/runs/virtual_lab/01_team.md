## Mechanics Scientist

**Mechanics Scientist, round 1**

I agree with the PI’s hysteron framing, but I would revise the mechanism, qualify the topology claim, and correct one network-C count.

**1. The \(\ell^2\) scaling is primarily geometric, not necessarily negative curvature.**  
For pair A, let \(\ell\) be `mount_l_cm` and let the driving attachment remain \(L=6.3\) cm. At a stop angle \(\theta_s\), horizontal torque balance gives approximately
\[
\gamma_{\rm th}=L\sin\theta_s+r_d+\frac{\tau_c(\theta_s)}{k_dL\cos\theta_s},
\]
so changes in threshold drive are direct images of the coupling torque at the stop, not directly of coupling energy curvature. If the target rotor moves between its stops, the coupling-spring length changes by \(O(\ell)\), hence the spring-force change is \(O(\ell)\); the torque change is lever arm times force, hence \(O(\ell^2)\). Likewise, changing the other rotor’s state changes its attachment position by \(O(\ell)\), producing a force change \(O(\ell)\) and a target torque \(O(\ell^2)\). This explains both the half-width collapse and the midpoint shifts without requiring negative coupling stiffness. Negative curvature is one possible source of the sign, but the data only establish the bilinear lever-arm/extension scaling.

The fitted \(L_c\approx7.9\) cm should therefore be treated as an empirical extrapolation, not a physical barrier-collapse length. It lies beyond the measured \(\ell\) range and near geometries where the attachment kinematics change qualitatively.

**2. A useful zero-coupling check.**  
The kinematic baseline hysteresis from the stops is approximately
\[
h_0\simeq L[\sin\theta_+-\sin\theta_-].
\]
Using the supplied stop angles gives about 7.5, 2.3, 4.0 and 7.9 cm for A-rotor 1, A-rotor 2, B-rotor 1 and B-rotor 2. The measured widths at maximum \(\ell\) are about 4.6, 1.1, 4.1 and 11.5 cm. Thus parallel coupling reduces width in pair A, crossed coupling leaves B-rotor 1 near baseline, and B-rotor 2 is nearly doubled. That is stronger evidence that coupling geometry redistributes torque differently between width and midpoint, not simply that crossing reverses an interaction sign.

**3. Pair B is not a clean crossed-versus-parallel test.**  
The measured sign difference is real: in pair B the midpoint shifts with the other rotor by \(+11.1\) cm (rotor 1) and \(+5.5\) cm (rotor 2), while the widths are almost unchanged (4.1→4.0 and 11.4→11.7 cm). In pair A at \(\ell=4.731\) cm, widths fall by about 2.6 and 0.6 cm and midpoints shift by \(-1.2\) and \(-4.4\) cm. But pairs A and B also differ in stop angles, mounting offsets, spring stiffness (0.07 versus 0.19 N/cm), rest lengths and crossing. Attributing the sign change to crossing alone is an assumption. A same-device parallel/crossed swap, or a geometry-based model with known attachment coordinates, would be needed to identify it.

**4. Network C: I do not reproduce the claimed reversal asymmetry.**  
Over one eight-event cycle, each rotor flips exactly twice: s1 at events 2 and 5; s2 at 6 and the closing transition; s3 at 3 and 8; s4 at 4 and 7. Across the 16 recorded events each rotor flips four times. So there is no measured “three s2 reversals versus four” asymmetry. The important feature remains the same-drive pairs at \(+5.8\) and \(-5.1\) cm. These are consistent with either near-degenerate independent thresholds or a sequential avalanche in which the first switch changes the coupling torque on the second. Ordering is preserved, but timing is unresolved.

**Conditional consequences.** If the \(\ell^2\) effect is geometric, changing coupling preload/rest length at fixed \(\ell\) should change the coefficients roughly proportionally to \(k_c\) and projection factors without changing the scaling. A negative-stiffness/buckling mechanism would instead show sign reversal or strong nonlinearity with preload. For network C, an avalanche predicts that the second same-drive threshold depends on which rotor switched first; near-degeneracy predicts independence. I recommend carrying forward the state-dependent hysteron model, but with \(\ell^2\) as geometric scaling and the cross-versus-parallel mechanism downgraded to conditional.

## Theory Scientist

**Theory Scientist, round 1**

I would keep the state-dependent hysteron representation, but I think the measured structure favors a **bias-dominated elastic interaction** more than the PI’s negative-curvature/barrier-collapse picture. Mechanics Scientist is right that \(\ell^2\) is geometric, but the key point is sharper: the coupling changes the two threshold midpoints much more than it changes the width when the other rotor’s state is changed.

Write the threshold as
\[
\gamma_i^{\pm}(s_j,\ell)=m_{i0}(\ell)+J_{ij}(\ell)s_j\pm h_i(\ell),
\]
with \(s_j=\pm1\). The measurements give three constraints.

1. **Width depends on \(\ell\), not much on \(s_j\).** In pair A, \(h_i\) falls by the same fractional amount for both rotors, from about 7.2 to 4.6 cm and 1.7 to 1.1 cm as \(\ell\) goes from 0 to 4.731 cm. But changing the other rotor changes \(h_i\) by at most 0.1 cm. In pair B the widths are nearly unchanged by the other state: 4.1 vs 4.0 cm and 11.4 vs 11.7 cm. This is a diagonal stiffness renormalization by \(\ell\), not a strong state-dependent barrier change.

2. **Midpoint bias is state-dependent.** At \(\ell=0\), pair A midpoints are almost independent of the other rotor. At \(\ell=4.731\) cm, the midpoint shifts are \(-1.2\) cm for rotor 1 and \(-4.4\) cm for rotor 2, implying \(J\approx-0.6\) and \(-2.2\) cm. Pair B gives positive shifts, \(J\approx+5.55\) and \(+2.75\) cm. The sign difference is real, but it is confounded with stiffness, stops, offsets, rest lengths and crossing. “Crossing reverses the sign” remains a conditional mechanism, not an identified one.

3. **A useful marginality parameter is \(\mu_i=|J_{ij}|/h_i\).** At maximum \(\ell\), pair A rotor 2 has \(\mu\approx2.0\), while rotor 1 has \(\mu\approx0.13\). Pair B rotor 1 has \(\mu\approx1.37\), rotor 2 \(\mu\approx0.24\). When \(\mu\gtrsim1\), one rotor’s state can force the other across its threshold; this is a natural route to same-drive avalanches in a coupled network.

**Proposed mechanism.** The coupling spring adds a torque offset at the stop, with lever-arm \(\times\) displacement scaling as \(\ell^2\). A symmetric parallel pair can produce a common fractional width reduction, while mounting asymmetry produces the bias \(J_{ij}s_j\). The physical mechanism is saddle-node loss of stability at the hard stops; the hysteron is an encoding, not the mechanism. Negative coupling curvature is unnecessary. The fitted \(L_c\approx7.9\) cm should not be read as a physical barrier-collapse length; it is an extrapolation beyond the measured \(\ell\) range.

**Network C.** I do not reproduce the PI’s “three versus four” asymmetry. Over one eight-event cycle each rotor flips exactly twice; over the 16 rows each flips four times. The data establish an exactly repeating ordered state cycle under cyclic drive, not necessarily a unique attracting orbit. The same-drive pairs at \(+5.8\) and \(-5.1\) cm remain the important feature. Notably, s1 moves first in both pairs, followed by s3 and s2 respectively. If these are avalanches, s1 is the trigger; if they are near-degenerate independent thresholds, s1 has no special role.

**Distinguishable consequences.** (i) If \(J\) is a geometric lever effect, reversing both coupling-attachment signs should leave \(J\) unchanged; reversing only one should flip its sign. (ii) A same-device parallel/crossed swap should flip the sign of \(J\) while leaving \(h_i\) roughly fixed if crossing is the cause. (iii) Blocking s1 in network C should shift the s2/s3 thresholds if the same-drive pairs are avalanches; it should leave them unchanged if they are independent near-degeneracies. Finer drive resolution would split true near-degeneracy but may not split a fast avalanche. (iv) Quasistatic conservative thresholds should be drive-rate independent; friction or inertia would produce rate dependence. The small calibration offsets do not establish rotor friction.

**Question I would put to the definition service:** are pair A’s two coupling springs symmetric about each pivot, and what is the sign convention of `x_mount_offsets` relative to the drive? That determines whether \(J\) should be even or odd in \(\ell\).
